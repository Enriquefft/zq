# zq Research Roadmap

> Parallel track to [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md). Engineering-first, paper-as-byproduct.
>
> The engineering roadmap ships features to users. This roadmap ships *the same
> work* organized to also produce defensible academic papers, with shared
> infrastructure (the harness, the parse-plan IR, the workload corpus) so the
> two tracks reinforce instead of compete.

---

## Operating Principles

1. **Engineering value gates everything.** Every research item must produce
   shippable engineering value. If an item only exists to fill a paper section,
   it is deferred until it earns its place by being engineering-useful.
2. **Papers are documentation of what worked, not the goal of the work.** The
   sequence is: build the system properly → measure honestly → write up the
   measurements that turned out to matter. Not: pick a paper title → build the
   thing the title implies.
3. **2 strong defensible papers > 5 weak ones.** We will not cargo-cult novelty
   to inflate the publication count. A paper ships when its thesis survives
   honest review of our own data, not before.
4. **Phase 0 (the harness) is non-negotiable.** No optimization is merged
   without an ablation row generated by the shared harness. The harness is
   *both* the engineering regression net *and* the paper's evaluation
   infrastructure — single source of truth.
5. **Production constraints are the evaluation methodology.** zq must remain
   jq-compatible and must not regress on any existing benchmark. This is
   stricter than what most research prototypes can claim and is itself a
   defensible reviewer-facing argument.
6. **Honest deferrals are first-class.** Items we are *not* doing, and *why*,
   are tracked here so future-us doesn't relitigate killed ideas.

## Paper 1 — JSONQBench: A Cross-System Benchmark for JSON Query Processors

### Thesis (one sentence)
No rigorous, controlled, cross-system benchmark exists for JSON query
processors; the published "X is faster than jq" comparisons are each
constructed by one system's authors against one or two competitors, on
non-standard workloads, with no shared methodology — and a principled
benchmark reveals a substantially more nuanced performance landscape than any
of those papers admit.

### Target venues
**Primary: VLDB Experiments-and-Analysis track** (PVLDB E&A). This track
explicitly exists for benchmark, measurement, and methodology contributions
that don't fit the novelty-first criteria of the main research track. The
acceptance bar for this track is "rigorous methodology + new insight," not
"new mechanism," which exactly matches what JSONQBench is. PVLDB uses rolling
monthly submissions, so we submit when the harness has produced its first
complete cross-system landscape pass with at least 3 documented surprises.

**Fallback: DBTest** (Workshop on Testing Database Systems, SIGMOD-co-located)
or **DaMoN** (Workshop on Data Management on New Hardware, also SIGMOD-
co-located). Both are workshop tier but have meaningful prestige in the
database community and are realistic targets for a first-time student author.
DBTest is the slightly better fit because the contribution is testing/
benchmarking methodology; DaMoN fits if we lean more into the SIMD and
hardware-aware angle.

### Contributions
- A reproducible benchmark harness covering jq, jaq, gojq, zq, simdjson (with
  a query layer), DuckDB `read_json`, ClickHouse JSON functions, and Polars
  JSON.
- A workload corpus spanning five domains (API responses, application logs,
  infrastructure configs, geospatial, scientific), with characterized
  dimensions: record width (5–500 fields), nesting depth (1–20), selectivity
  (0.1%–100%), file size (1 MB–100 GB), schema homogeneity.
- A microbench attribution model splitting per-record cost into parse, lookup,
  predicate, serialization, and coordination phases — so cross-system
  comparisons attribute *why* one system wins, not just *that* it does.
- A scalability matrix: throughput as a function of cores (1–64), file size,
  record size, selectivity, nesting depth.
- Surfaced surprises: at least 3 documented, explained "this should win but
  doesn't" or "this should lose but doesn't" findings — these are what give
  the paper its analytical hook beyond "here is a leaderboard."

### Why it's defensible
- The literature gap is real and named: every prior paper compared to its
  immediate predecessor; nobody published a controlled cross-system landscape.
- Benchmark/experiments papers have their own venues with explicit acceptance
  criteria — we don't have to compete with novelty-driven systems papers.
- Reproducibility is the entire point of these venues; we ship the harness,
  the corpus, and the result database.
- Zero risk to the engineering codebase — Phase 0 work is purely additive
  infrastructure.

### Expected reviewer attacks
- *"This is just running other systems and reporting numbers."* → Mitigated by
  the attribution model and the explained surprises. A leaderboard alone is a
  workshop paper; an attributed analytical landscape is a real contribution.
- *"Workload selection is biased toward zq's strengths."* → Mitigated by
  including workloads we *expect to lose on* (identity queries on tiny
  records, REPL-style multi-query workloads, heterogeneous schemas), and by
  having domain coverage from real-world dataset sources, not generated.
- *"Why these systems and not <X>?"* → Defensible inclusion criteria stated
  upfront: "command-line, file-or-pipe, query-language-driven JSON processors,
  plus the analytical engines that have first-class JSON ingestion as a
  comparison anchor."

### Engineering work that produces it
**Phase 0 in the phase plan below.** Every artifact produced for this paper
is also infrastructure the engineering track needs:
- Multi-system runner generalizes the existing `benchmarks/run_all.sh` to N
  systems instead of 4.
- Microbench attribution extends the in-memory `microbench.zig` referenced in
  prior notes (must be reconstructed — not currently in the tree).
- Workload corpus replaces the single synthetic `huge.jsonl` generator with a
  curated multi-domain set, documented and licensable.
- Scalability sweep matrix becomes the standard CI ablation table for every
  subsequent optimization.

### Experimental requirements
- Reproducible execution environment (Nix flake already exists; extend to
  pin every benchmarked system at a specific version with checksums).
- Statistical rigor: hyperfine warmups, ≥10 runs per cell, report median + p99
  + standard error, document outlier policy.
- Hardware coverage: at minimum one x86 (AVX2 + AVX-512 if available) and one
  ARM (NEON). Scaling sweeps on a 64-core machine.
- Storage of results in machine-readable form (JSON or Parquet), not just
  Markdown — so future papers can re-query.

### Status
Not started. No blockers. **This is the first concrete engineering deliverable
of the entire research roadmap.**

### Open questions
- Workload corpus licensing: which datasets can we redistribute? Where we
  can't, what's the substitute?
- Hardware access: do we have a 64-core machine for scaling sweeps, or do we
  need to source one?
- Inclusion criteria for systems: do we include serverless / cloud JSON tools
  (Athena, BigQuery)? Probably no — different cost model — but we should say
  why explicitly.

## Paper 2 — Compiler-Driven Query-Aware Parsing in a Parallel JSON VM

### Thesis (one sentence)
A bytecode query compiler that emits a *parse plan* consumed by a parallel
SIMD parser unifies projection and predicate pushdown under a single IR,
turning the parser into a target of the compiler rather than a black box
upstream of it, and recovering the dominant share of parse-time cost on
selective queries over wide records — without regressing the unprojected
baseline.

### Target venues
**Primary: PVLDB main research track** (the canonical academic systems home;
rolling monthly submissions remove deadline pressure for a first-time student
author and let us submit when the work is genuinely ready). **PVLDB-Industrial
is hard-out**: its CFP requires at least one author with non-academic
affiliation, which we don't have. The main research track is the realistic
ambition.

**Fallback: EDBT main track.** EDBT is the European top-tier database
conference, slightly below SIGMOD/VLDB in prestige but well-respected and
historically friendlier to systems contributions from non-prestigious
affiliations. Submission cycle is annual (mid-year deadline).

**Last resort: DaMoN workshop.** If both main tracks reject, the technical
contribution still has a workshop home with meaningful database-community
prestige. Use only after at least one main-track attempt.

**Why this ordering for a first-time student author:** the rolling-submission
model at PVLDB makes the high-prestige aim genuinely viable — we can submit,
get detailed reviewer feedback, revise based on the feedback, and resubmit
the next month. That feedback loop is the single most valuable thing
PVLDB offers a first-time author. It also means a rejection at the main
track is not a binary "paper failed" outcome — it's reviewer feedback that
the next submission incorporates. The fallback chain only kicks in if we
get a "reject for venue fit" rather than "revise and resubmit" decision.

### Contributions
1. **The parse-plan IR.** A compact, statically-determined intermediate
   representation produced by the query compiler's existing fuse pass and
   consumed by the parser. One IR, multiple consumers, no duplicated
   field-access analysis. The IR is the named contribution; projection and
   predicate pushdown are *instances* of using it.
2. **Projection pushdown in a chunk-parallel SIMD parser.** Mison's level-
   selective bitmap technique extended to per-worker parse plans broadcast at
   compile time, with parallel-aware boundary detection that remains required
   for chunked reading and bounds the achievable savings.
3. **Predicate pushdown into the parser.** dsJSON's projection-tree concept
   reformulated as a bytecode-VM compilation target: the compiler emits
   parse-and-filter plans for `select()` over scalar fields; the parser
   short-circuits as soon as the predicate field is materialized.
4. **Output-equivalence preservation under parallel parse-time filtering.** A
   not-obvious correctness story: parse-time filtering must produce the same
   output stream as execute-time filtering under arbitrary worker schedules.
5. **An honest characterization of when the technique helps and when it
   doesn't.** Workload sweeps from JSONQBench (Paper 1) showing the regimes
   where projection wins, where predicate pushdown wins, where they compound,
   and where they break even or lose (identity queries, narrow records, deep
   nesting, heterogeneous schemas, small inputs).

### Why it's defensible
- The integration point is real and previously unbuilt: no prior system
  combines compiler-driven query-aware parsing with a *parallel*
  *bytecode-VM* JSON query processor. Mison was single-threaded and
  predicate-free; Sparser was single-threaded and projection-free; dsJSON was
  distributed SQL, not a bytecode VM; simdjson on-demand is lazy but not
  compiler-driven. There is a real white space.
- The IR framing reframes "we wired three things together" into "the parser
  is a compile target, here's the IR." This sidesteps the fatal "no novelty,
  just integration" reviewer attack.
- The characterization of *where the technique loses* is itself a contribution
  — most papers on parser optimizations hide this.
- It runs in production code, gated by hundreds of tests, on real workloads.
  This is harder to dismiss than a research prototype.

### Expected reviewer attacks
- *"This is just Mison + dsJSON wired into a different system."* → Mitigated
  by (a) the IR as a named first-class contribution, (b) the parallel-context
  design problems neither prior work addressed (per-worker plan distribution,
  output ordering preservation, boundary-detection floor), and (c) the
  bytecode-VM target which neither prior work supported.
- *"Mison already showed projection pushdown wins."* → Mitigated by being
  explicit that we are *not* claiming projection pushdown is novel; we are
  claiming the integration into a parallel bytecode VM is novel, and the
  ablation is novel.
- *"Why doesn't this beat dsJSON / Mison head-to-head?"* → If it doesn't
  (likely on some workloads — Mison's speculation wins on homogeneous JSONL),
  we say so, and explain why. Honest comparisons are stronger than fake wins.
- *"What about the unprojected baseline?"* → Mitigated by zero-overhead
  comptime specialization plus the gated regression CI policy. We can show
  the ablation table includes the no-regression cell.
- *"JSON validation semantics?"* → Mitigated by an explicit decision (see
  open questions) documented in the paper.

### Engineering work that produces it
- Phase 1: parse-plan IR as a no-op (the load-bearing artifact)
- Phase 3: projection pushdown
- Phase 4: predicate pushdown
- Plus selected unconditional engineering wins from Phase 2 that contribute
  ablation rows: object key indexing, cached length, COW strings — *not* as
  paper pillars, but as supporting evidence under "the parser specializes its
  output representation per query."

### Experimental requirements
- Phase 0 harness must exist (Paper 1 infrastructure).
- Workload sweep across the JSONQBench corpus, with every cell in the
  ablation table including a no-projection baseline.
- Comparison against Mison (or its closest open-source reproduction), dsJSON
  (if any artifact is available — likely we have to reimplement projection
  trees on top of simdjson as a competitive baseline), and simdjson on-demand.
- Microbench attribution showing parse-time savings line up with the
  predicted ceiling (40–55% of current parse cost, not 73%).
- Statistical: same rigor as Paper 1.

### Status
Not started. Blocked on Paper 1 infrastructure (phase 0).

### Open questions (must be answered before phase 1 starts)
- **JSON validation semantics.** Default = full validation (jq-compatible,
  ~30–50% of projection win returned)? Opt-in fast mode? Both? *Decision
  required before IR design.*
- **Exact target venue.** VLDB/SIGMOD main track is the ambition; PVLDB-
  Industrial is the safer fallback. The framing of the IR section differs
  between them — main track wants more mechanism novelty, industrial wants
  more "we shipped this to users."
- **Mison reproduction.** Is there an open-source Mison we can run, or do we
  have to build a competitive baseline? This affects 2–4 weeks of work.

## Paper 3 — Closing the Streaming Gap: Parallel I/O for JSON Query Processing

### Thesis (one sentence)
Streaming JSON processors are 3.3× slower than mmap'd file mode for the same
work, and a measurement-driven redesign of the I/O path — multi-threaded
reading, zero-copy batch handoff, broadcast-on-drain coordination, ring-buffer
batch pool — closes the gap without sacrificing the streaming mode's bounded
memory footprint.

### Target venues
**Primary: USENIX ATC short paper.** ATC is the most natural fit for a
measurement study + principled fix paper, and the short-paper format (5–6
pages) matches the contribution size honestly. Annual deadline, single
shot per year, but the content is well-aligned and the bar for short
papers is meaningfully lower than for full ATC papers.

**Fallback: DaMoN workshop.** Same SIGMOD-co-located workshop as Paper 1's
fallback; the streaming/file-mode I/O contribution fits its scope.

**Decision deferral:** the venue call is reconsidered after Phase 5
measurement. If the residual-floor characterization throws up unexpected
sub-causes, the contribution may grow into a full ATC paper or even a
full PVLDB submission. If it stays "the four obvious things plus a clean
analytical writeup," ATC short paper is the right fit.

### Contributions
1. **A measurement study.** Phase-by-phase decomposition of where the 3.3×
   gap goes: single I/O thread, per-batch `dupe()` allocation, single-thread
   wakeup on queue drain, mmap zero-copy advantage. Numbers, not hand-waving.
2. **A principled redesign.** Multi-threaded I/O with bounded read-ahead,
   zero-copy batch handoff via a ring buffer, broadcast wakeup on drain, and
   the back-pressure protocol that holds memory bounded while keeping all
   workers busy.
3. **An evaluation showing the gap closes** without regressing memory (the
   streaming mode currently uses ~7 MB; the redesign must hold under 2× of
   that under all workload conditions).
4. **A characterization of the residual gap.** Even after the fix, mmap will
   beat streaming by *some* margin (page-cache priming, no syscall cost). We
   characterize that residual and explain why it is the irreducible floor.

### Why it's defensible
- The current 3.3× gap is documented in the project memory and independently
  reproducible. The measurement is unambiguous.
- The redesign uses well-understood techniques (multi-threaded readers,
  ring buffers, broadcast wakeup) — the contribution is *the analysis* and
  *the integrated fix*, not novel mechanisms. Systems venues reward this.
- It is independent of Paper 2's mechanisms — different code path, different
  measurements, different reviewers — so its acceptance is uncorrelated with
  Paper 2's.
- It ships value to every streaming user immediately. It is engineering-first
  by construction.

### Expected reviewer attacks
- *"This is just well-known techniques applied."* → Mitigated by framing the
  paper as a *measurement study*. The contribution is the analysis of where
  the gap was hiding and the residual-floor characterization, not the
  mechanisms.
- *"Why is mmap still faster?"* → We answer this in the residual section.
  Honesty wins.
- *"Why not just use mmap then?"* → Streaming mode is not optional —
  pipe inputs cannot be mmap'd. The gap is real and matters.

### Engineering work that produces it
- Phase 5 in the phase plan. Largely independent of Phases 1–4.

### Experimental requirements
- Before/after on the existing benchmark scenarios (`04_streaming.sh`).
- New scenarios specifically for streaming I/O: small/medium/large file
  piped, varying record sizes, varying query selectivity, varying number of
  cores (the broadcast wakeup matters more at high core counts).
- Memory bound verification — peak RSS must remain under the documented
  ceiling.
- Comparison against mmap mode as the upper bound; characterize the
  irreducible residual gap.

### Status
Not started. Independent of Paper 2; can be run in parallel with phase 3+.

### Open questions
- Is there enough material for a *full* paper, or is this a USENIX-ATC short
  paper? Decision after we have the measurement breakdown — if there are
  unexpected sub-causes, it grows; if it's just "the four obvious things,"
  it's a short paper.
- Is there a non-streaming I/O comparison we should include (e.g., Linux
  io_uring, posix_fadvise patterns)?

## Paper 4 — Query-Aware Memory Management for JSON Processing *(speculative)*

### Thesis (one sentence, draft)
Per-query memory specialization — eager `madvise(MADV_DONTNEED)` page release,
copy-on-write strings driven by query analysis, length caching, optional
per-chunk string deduplication — holds high-throughput parallel JSON
processing under 0.5× input size for the workloads where memory is the
bottleneck, without resorting to engine-mode workarounds.

### Why speculative
The constituent techniques are real engineering wins (some already done —
the madvise work has shipped per project memory), but it is not yet clear
whether they cohere into a paper-shaped story or whether they are best
treated as a section in Paper 2 ("the parser specializes its output
representation per query"). The decision is **made by data**: after Phases 2,
3, and 6, if the cumulative memory story has at least three independent
techniques each with >20% impact and a coherent compiler-driven framing, this
becomes a paper. Otherwise it folds into Paper 2.

### Target venues (if it ships)
**DaMoN** or **VLDB short paper**. Memory-management papers have a hard time
at SIGMOD/VLDB main track unless they also bring novel mechanisms.

### Status
Speculative. Decision deferred to post-Phase-6 data review.

## Paper 5 — Per-Chunk Adaptive Execution *(deeply speculative)*

### Thesis (one sentence, draft)
Chunk granularity is the natural adaptation unit for parallel JSON
processing — coarser than Eddies' per-tuple routing (which has fatal overhead
for sub-microsecond per-record costs) and finer than Spark AQE's per-stage
adaptation (which has nothing to materialize at). A chunk-level adaptive
controller can switch parsing strategy, chunk size, and worker policy
mid-query based on first-N-chunk statistics.

### Why deeply speculative
The roadmap explicitly tags this "Profile first." Adaptive execution only
pays off when workloads are heterogeneous within a single run *and* the
adaptation overhead is below the savings. We do not yet know whether real
workloads are heterogeneous enough. Phase 0's workload characterization
should answer this empirically.

**Decision rule:** if Phase 0's heterogeneous-workload data shows that
fixed-strategy execution is leaving >20% on the table on at least 3
workloads, this becomes a paper. Otherwise it stays unbuilt.

### Target venues (if it ships)
SIGMOD or VLDB. The adaptive execution literature is a respected line; a
chunk-granularity contribution could land at the main track.

### Status
Deeply speculative. Decision deferred to post-Phase-0 data review.

## Phase Plan

The phases are ordered by dependency, not by paper. Each phase produces
shippable engineering value and (where applicable) a contribution to one or
more papers. Phases that don't produce paper material exist to enable
phases that do.

> Effort estimates are in calendar weeks of focused work. They are estimates,
> not deadlines. The user said no deadline; the goal is "no waste."

### Phase 0 — Evaluation harness *(4–6 weeks)*

**Engineering deliverable:**
- Multi-system benchmark runner extending `benchmarks/run_all.sh` from 4
  systems (jq/jaq/gojq/zq) to 8 (add simdjson + custom query layer, DuckDB
  `read_json`, ClickHouse JSON, Polars JSON).
- Microbench attribution tool (rebuild `src/microbench.zig` — referenced in
  prior notes but not currently in tree) splitting per-record cost into
  parse, lookup, predicate, serialization, coordination phases.
- Workload corpus replacing the single `huge.jsonl` generator with five
  curated domains: API responses, application logs, infrastructure configs,
  geospatial, scientific. Each with documented provenance, license,
  characterization (record width, depth, string ratio, schema stability).
- Scalability sweep harness producing the cross-product matrix of cores,
  file sizes, record sizes, selectivity, nesting depth. Results stored in
  machine-readable form.
- CI gate: every PR that touches a hot path runs the relevant subset of the
  harness and posts the ablation row.

**Paper deliverable:** Paper 1 (JSONQBench) first draft can begin once the
harness is complete and has produced its first cross-system landscape pass.

**Blockers:**
- Workload corpus licensing.
- Hardware access for 64-core scaling sweeps.

**Why this is first:**
- Zero baseline risk (purely additive infrastructure).
- Required by every subsequent phase as the regression gate.
- Independent of every design decision in Phases 1+.
- Produces the first paper without touching the parser or the compiler.

### Phase 1 — Parse-plan IR as a strict no-op *(1–2 weeks)*

**Engineering deliverable:**
- Design document for the parse-plan IR (the load-bearing artifact for Paper 2).
- Extension of the existing fuse pass to emit a "materialize everything"
  parse plan as a side artifact alongside bytecode.
- Parser-side consumer of the plan, gated by comptime specialization so the
  no-projection case compiles to the *current* SIMD parser byte-for-byte.
- CI gate: zero benchmark regression vs. Phase 0 baseline on every existing
  scenario, especially identity queries (`.`, `keys`, `to_entries`).

**Paper deliverable:** none directly. This phase exists to make Phases 3–4
implementable cleanly.

**Blockers:**
- JSON validation semantics decision (must be locked in before the IR is
  designed, because it determines what the IR encodes about skipped regions).
- Phase 0 must be complete (regression gate).

**Why this is second:**
- Highest-leverage design decision in the entire research roadmap.
- If the IR is wrong, every subsequent paper inherits the wrong abstraction.
- Independently shippable (a no-op IR is still a real engineering deliverable
  in the sense that future PRs build on it).

### Phase 2 — Unconditional engineering wins *(parallelizable, 2–4 weeks total)*

**Engineering deliverables (each independent, each lands separately):**
- **Object key indexing** (Track D). Compact hash table for objects with
  >N keys, built during parsing.
- **Cached array/object length** (Track D). Element count stored in tape;
  `length` returns cached value.
- **Constant folding** (Track C). Compile-time arithmetic and boolean
  simplification.
- **Extended fuse pass** (Track C). `key | has` → `key_exists`,
  `key | length` → `key_length`, `key | type` → `key_type`.

**Paper deliverable:** ablation rows for Paper 2's "the parser specializes
its output representation per query" supporting evidence section. Not
pillars — supporting rows.

**Why this is third:**
- None depend on the parse-plan IR landing first; can run in parallel with
  Phases 0 and 1.
- All produce immediate engineering value.
- Each provides an ablation row for Paper 2 without committing to anything
  paper-shaped.

### Phase 3 — Projection pushdown *(3–6 weeks)*

**Engineering deliverable:**
- Per-level SIMD bitmap construction (colon, comma, brace) gated by the
  parse plan's required-levels set.
- Tape construction skipped for unneeded fields.
- Workload sweep showing where it wins, where it breaks even, where it loses.
- CI gate: still no regression on identity / unprojected queries.

**Paper deliverable:** Paper 2 main result.

**Blockers:**
- Phases 0 and 1 must be complete.

### Phase 4 — Predicate pushdown *(3–4 weeks)*

**Engineering deliverable:**
- Compiler emits parse-and-filter plans for `select(.f op v)` over scalar
  top-level fields.
- Parser short-circuits as soon as the predicate field is materialized.
- Output-equivalence verification under arbitrary worker schedules
  (covered by existing tests + new property tests for the parallel case).

**Paper deliverable:** Paper 2 second result, compounding with projection
pushdown on selective queries over wide records.

**Blockers:**
- Phase 3 must be complete (shares the parse-plan IR).

### Phase 5 — Streaming/file gap closure *(2–4 weeks)*

**Engineering deliverable:**
- Multi-threaded I/O with bounded read-ahead.
- Zero-copy batch handoff via ring buffer.
- Broadcast wakeup on queue drain.
- Memory-bound verification.

**Paper deliverable:** Paper 3 main result.

**Why this is fifth (not earlier):**
- Independent of Phases 1–4 (different code path).
- Could move earlier if Phase 0 throws up streaming-related findings that
  warrant it. The order is recommended, not strict.

### Phase 6 — COW strings + memory wins *(2–3 weeks)*

**Engineering deliverable:**
- COW strings from input buffer for unescaped strings (gated by parse plan
  knowing the query is read-only).
- Any other memory wins discovered during phases 0–5.

**Paper deliverable:** Either supporting evidence in Paper 2, or the seed
of Paper 4 (decision made by data after this phase).

### Phase 7 — Speculative items *(only if data justifies)*

**Items potentially in scope:**
- Per-chunk adaptive execution (Track B) — only if Phase 0 shows
  heterogeneous workloads cost >20%.
- Speculative schema caching (Mison-style) — only if Phase 0 shows real
  JSONL schema homogeneity > some threshold.
- Sparser-style raw byte pre-filtering — only if Phase 0 shows high-selectivity
  filter workloads exist in real data.
- Monomorphic selector specialization — only if Phase 0 shows
  filter-dominated queries cost >X%.

Each is a 2–4 week phase. None are committed up front.

## Cross-Cutting Infrastructure

These artifacts are shared across all papers and phases. They are the
single-source-of-truth tools that make the engineering-first strategy work.

### The benchmark harness *(Phase 0)*
- One runner, many systems.
- Result database in machine-readable form.
- Reproducible execution environment (Nix flake).
- Statistical methodology documented and enforced.

### The parse-plan IR *(Phase 1)*
- Single artifact emitted by the fuse pass.
- Consumed by the parser, the predicate evaluator, the data-structure
  selector.
- No duplicated field-access analysis anywhere in the codebase (CLAUDE.md
  single-source-of-truth requirement).
- Comptime specialization makes the no-op case zero overhead.

### The workload corpus *(Phase 0)*
- Five domains, documented provenance and license.
- Characterized along the dimensions the papers ablate over.

## Killed Items (and Why)

These are items we are explicitly **not** doing, captured here so we don't
relitigate them. Each kill is reversible if data justifies revisiting.

- **The original mega-paper.** Tries to do four things in one paper, none
  cleanly. Replaced by the three-paper portfolio above.
- **On-demand lazy materialization** as a paper track. Subsumed by projection
  pushdown — they solve the same problem, projection is simpler.
- **CSE in the bytecode VM.** JSON queries are rarely complex enough; the
  expression DAG cost exceeds the savings.
- **Tail call optimization** for `def` functions. Almost nobody writes
  recursive `def` in jq; not a real workload.
- **Cost model for mode selection** as a paper. A few `if` statements on file
  size and query type get 95% of the way; a formal model is over-engineering
  and not novel.
- **Inline small strings.** High blast radius (changes tape layout) for
  marginal cache gain.
- **Type-aware arithmetic opcodes** (`add_int`, `add_float`, `add_string`).
  5–8% gain on queries that barely exist in jq workloads.
- **Compiler-informed data structures as a paper pillar.** Folded into Paper 2
  as supporting ablation rows. Not a third leg of the contribution.
- **Sparser-style raw byte pre-filtering as a committed paper track.**
  Engineering work only if Phase 0 data shows the workloads exist.

## Decision Log

A running log of the strategic calls made on this roadmap. Each decision
lists the alternatives considered and why the chosen path won. Updated as
new decisions are made.

### 2026-04-07 — Engineering-first, paper-as-byproduct
**Alternatives:** Paper-first prototype; engineering and paper in parallel as
equals; engineering first with paper as byproduct.
**Chosen:** Engineering first with paper as byproduct.
**Why:** CLAUDE.md production-quality bar makes paper-first impossible
without violating it. Test suite + fast dev + no deadline make
engineering-first feasible. Real systems papers (DuckDB, Velox, ClickHouse,
simdjson) all came from production systems; this is a respected path.
**Failure mode designed against:** the paper never gets written because the
experimental harness wasn't there in time. Mitigation: Phase 0 (the harness)
is non-negotiable and goes first; every optimization PR generates its
ablation row at merge time.

### 2026-04-07 — Three-paper portfolio replaces the original mega-paper
**Alternatives:** Stick with the original combined paper; slice into 4–5
papers; slice into 2.
**Chosen:** Three definite papers (JSONQBench, Compiler-Driven Parsing,
Streaming Gap) + one speculative (Memory) + two deeply speculative
(Adaptive, Speculation).
**Why:** The original combined four contributions in one paper, three of
which are independently strong enough to carry their own. Slicing produces
papers with clear theses, independent reviewer pools, and uncorrelated
acceptance risk. Slicing into 4–5 over-fragments (projection alone is too
thin; predicate alone is too thin); 2 leaves the streaming work as an
appendix it doesn't deserve. Three is the right cleavage.

### 2026-04-07 — Compiler-informed data structures fold into Paper 2 as
supporting evidence, not a paper pillar
**Alternatives:** Make it the third pillar of Paper 2; spin it out as
Paper 4 immediately; fold it as supporting evidence; defer the decision.
**Chosen:** Fold as supporting evidence; spin out to Paper 4 only if
Phase 6 data shows enough cumulative material.
**Why:** As a pillar it dilutes Paper 2's narrative (the IR + projection +
predicate is a tight story; adding "and also some data structures" weakens
it). As an immediate paper it's too thin. As supporting evidence under "the
parser specializes its output representation per query" it strengthens
Paper 2 without diluting it.

### 2026-04-07 — Per-chunk adaptive execution and Sparser-style filtering
are deeply speculative
**Alternatives:** Commit to them now; defer entirely; gate on data.
**Chosen:** Gate on Phase 0 workload characterization data.
**Why:** Both are workload-conditional. Without data showing the workloads
they target actually exist in real-world data, building them is paper-only
work that violates the engineering-first principle. The roadmap already
tags them "Profile first" — this just makes that tag concrete.

### 2026-04-07 — Validation semantics: simdjson middle path + opt-in strict
**Alternatives:** (A) Full validation always (preserve full jq compat at the
cost of ~50–70% of the projection win); (B) simdjson on-demand's documented
middle path (validate boundaries always, content only when accessed; ~90–95%
of projection win; rare edge-case divergence from jq); (C) maximum lazy
(validate only enough to find record boundaries; significant divergence; no
published precedent).
**Chosen:** (B) as the default + opt-in `--strict-validation` flag that
flips the policy to (A) for users who need bit-exact jq compatibility.
**Why:** The expensive validation cost is content validation (number
parsing, escape processing, char-by-char UTF-8 — roughly 80% of validation
cost). Structural validation is cheap because it shares the same SIMD bitmap
pass as projection pushdown. Option B is therefore close to a free lunch
in performance terms, and its compatibility divergence is bounded to the
"malformed contents in a never-touched field" case which is vanishingly
rare in practice. Option B also has a defensible published rationale we
can cite (simdjson on-demand documented design), which sidesteps having
to invent and defend our own validation policy. The opt-in flag preserves
strict-jq behavior for the small set of users who actually depend on it.
Crucially, jq itself does not implement spec-strict JSON validation (it
quietly accepts leading zeros, trailing decimals, `infinite`/`nan`); the
proposed jq `--strict` flag has been open in jqlang/jq for years without
landing. So Option A's "preserve jq compat" claim is itself fuzzy.
**Implication for Phase 1 IR design:** the parse-plan IR carries a
`validation_mode` field with two states (`middle_path`, `strict`). The
parser's structural validation path is shared between modes; only the
content-validation hooks differ. The default value is `middle_path`; the
`--strict-validation` CLI flag flips it to `strict`. The opt-in flag is a
*configuration*, not a separate code path — single source of truth.

### 2026-04-07 — Paper 2 venue strategy: PVLDB main → EDBT → DaMoN
**Context:** Project lead is a last-year college student. PVLDB Industrial
track requires at least one author with non-academic affiliation, which is
not available. The question reduces to: what venue ordering maximizes
publication success odds for a first-time student author with the
contributions in Paper 2?
**Alternatives:** (a) PVLDB main only (high prestige, high risk); (b) EDBT
main only (good prestige, lower risk); (c) DaMoN workshop only (high odds,
lowest prestige); (d) multi-venue cascade with PVLDB main as the primary.
**Chosen:** (d) multi-venue cascade. Primary: PVLDB main research track.
Fallback: EDBT main. Last resort: DaMoN workshop.
**Why:** The single most valuable thing PVLDB offers a first-time student
author is the **rolling-submission monthly review cycle**. Unlike fixed-
deadline conferences where rejection means waiting a year, PVLDB lets us
submit, get detailed reviewer feedback within ~weeks, revise, and resubmit
the next month. For a first-time author this feedback loop is more valuable
than the prestige itself — even a rejection from PVLDB main is detailed
expert feedback on framing, evaluation, and positioning that strengthens
the eventual landing venue. EDBT and DaMoN are positioned as fallbacks that
inherit the PVLDB-improved manuscript, not as parallel submissions.
**Why not just aim at the easy venue first:** workshop-only papers are real
but have measurably less impact on PhD applications and industry recruiting
than main-track papers. For a student trying to maximize career upside, one
ambitious main-track attempt + fallback chain dominates "play it safe and
aim only at workshops."
**Why not aim higher than PVLDB:** PVLDB *is* the high aim. SIGMOD/VLDB are
the same tier; OSDI/ATC are systems venues with a different reviewer culture
and a different framing requirement. PVLDB main is the right ceiling.
**Implication for paper framing:** Paper 2 is written to PVLDB main's
acceptance criteria from the start. The parse-plan-IR-as-compile-target
reframe is non-negotiable for this venue — without it, the "we wired three
things together" reviewer attack lands and the paper dies. The IR is the
named first-class contribution; projection and predicate pushdown are
*instances* of using it.

## How This Roadmap Stays Honest

- **Decision log gets updated, not rewritten.** Strategy changes are appended
  with reasons, not silently revised.
- **Killed items stay killed** until data justifies revisiting. Sticking to
  killed-item discipline is how we avoid scope creep.
- **Speculative papers stay speculative** until the data review at the end
  of their gating phase. We do not pre-commit to write Paper 4 or 5; we
  decide after seeing the numbers.
- **Phase 0 is sacred.** No phase later than 0 starts before the harness can
  measure its result. Any temptation to skip the harness is a sign we're
  drifting toward paper-first.
- **The engineering roadmap (`ROADMAP.md`) and the research roadmap (this
  file) cross-link.** Items that are on both are tagged on both. Items that
  exist only here are explicitly research-only and have to earn their way
  into the engineering roadmap by being Implement-tagged.
